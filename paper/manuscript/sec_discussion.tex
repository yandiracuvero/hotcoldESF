\section{Discussion}\label{sec:conc}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

We have proposed a procedure to map covariate-adjusted hot and cold spots of urban pest reports and to turn the map into a priority list. It combines a test for residual spatial structure, an estimate of the spatial component by eigenvector spatial filtering with smooth eigenvectors and a ridge penalty, a permutation band that labels each cell, and a ranking of the hot cells. We compared it with $G_i^*$, local Moran's $I$ and rankings without spatial smoothing in a simulation study, and applied it to cockroach sightings in Madrid.

\paragraph{No method is best in every setting.} The simulation study shows trade-offs rather than a single winner. With strong clusters, ESF, $G_i^*$ and local Moran's $I$ are equally precise. With weak compact clusters, $G_i^*$ is the most precise, because its fixed neighbourhood matches the cluster size. When clusters of different sizes coexist, ESF reaches the small clusters more often, because the eigenvectors represent patterns at several scales. When the clusters are smaller than a neighbourhood of cells, ranking the residuals without smoothing is the most precise. The choice therefore depends on what the user expects the clusters to look like. We keep ESF as the main method because it adjusts the covariates and the spatial component in one model, so the covariate coefficients are corrected for residual spatial correlation (Section~\ref{sec:app-covariates}); it gives a continuous map of the excess with a band for every cell; and it detects hot and cold areas at several scales without fixing a neighbourhood. Local statistics on the residuals are a useful complement and are cheap to compute, and we recommend reporting them alongside.

\paragraph{Test before mapping.} The step that matters most for error control is the test of Step~1, not the choice of local statistic. Without it, $G_i^*$ labels about 5\% of a map without clusters as hot, as expected from its level; with it, ESF, $G_i^*$ and local Moran's $I$ label at most 2.0\% of such maps. The test can be placed in front of any local statistic. A false discovery rate correction of the local tests \citep{benjamini1995controlling} gives the most precise labels but misses most cells of weak clusters, which for a control campaign is usually the more costly error.

\paragraph{The covariates define the hot spot.} A hot spot is only defined relative to the covariates in the model. In the simulation, when the environmental covariate was not given to the model, every method placed most of its priority list in the area explained by that covariate. In Madrid, exposure alone explains 80\% of the variation in log reports, and adding environmental risk factors and rat reports changes which cells are hot. Applied to rats with the same covariates, the procedure gives a map that shares little with the cockroach map, although the raw counts of the two species are almost the same map. Practitioners should choose the covariates according to the question: M1 identifies areas with more reports than their population predicts, M2 areas with more reports than their environment also predicts, and M3 areas where cockroaches are reported more than rats.

\paragraph{From the map to the priority list.} Evaluated on the following summer, ranking by counts captures the most reports, because reports follow exposure and exposure is stable. Covariate-adjusted rankings target a different quantity, the excess over what the covariates predict. For this target, the residuals of single cells were more persistent than the smoothed map, and ranking them captured more of the next summer's excess than ESF. This agrees with scenario S of the simulation study and suggests a two-level use of the output: the hot zones of the procedure indicate where to act, and the residuals within each zone indicate the order.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Reports measure sightings by citizens, not pest abundance; the exposure variables and rat reports reduce, but do not remove, the influence of reporting habits, and M3 also removes the risk factors that rats and cockroaches share. The covariates were compiled once and are used for all four summers, and a third of the 1 km cells contain no residential address point, so their covariates are taken from their neighbours. The variables recorded during the field inspections of the vector control unit were excluded from the main model because they may follow the reports. The test of Step~1 assumes that the covariate effects are linear on the log scale; when they are not, the residuals keep part of the covariate pattern and the test rejects more often than its level (19--32\% of datasets in scenario C). A spline for each covariate removes this, at the cost of power with weak clusters, and can be used as a sensitivity analysis. The results depend on the cell size \citep{openshaw1984modifiable}: in Madrid the main zones appear with cells of 500 m, 1 km and 2 km, but their extent changes, so the cell size should be chosen with the intervention in mind. Finally, the procedure models $\log(1+w)$ with a Gaussian approximation, which is adequate for the counts in our application but not for very sparse counts.

\paragraph{Extensions.} The ridge penalty on the smooth eigenvectors is close to the random-effects formulation of ESF \citep{murakami2015random}, which gives each eigenvector a variance that depends on its eigenvalue; that formulation, and a Poisson likelihood for the counts, are natural extensions. The persistence of the hot cells across summers suggests a space--time version, in which the spatial component evolves over time and the priority list is updated as new reports arrive. Because the procedure needs only counts per area, covariates and a contiguity matrix, it can be applied to other allocation problems in which resources must be placed where events exceed what local characteristics predict.
